# The Sundered Crown — Lore Bible

## 1. The realm at a glance
Hallan is a kingdom of river valleys, hill country, southern ports and cold northern fen, ruled for two hundred years from the city of Wyckholm. Nineteen days before the story begins, King Aldwin Ardane died in his bed at fifty-one, officially of the sweating fever. His only son, Prince Edmer, died five years ago. Aldwin named no heir.

Since then the Regency has held the realm together: a council led by Osric Dunmore, the late king's chancellor, a man of no great house who rules through ledgers, favours and fear. The Regency has called a **Council of Claims** to meet in the Council Chamber of the Ashen Hall on **day 40** (the first day of spring). There the great houses will put forward their claims, and the Grey Chapter will declare which claim is lawful. Until then every house is counting swords, buying votes and looking for leverage.

What most people believe: the king died of fever; the prince burned in a hunting-lodge fire; Lord Edric Calder has the best claim by blood; the Regency will hand over power once a king is chosen. Almost all of this is wrong or incomplete (see §9).

## 2. History (what an educated person knows)
- **The Founding (212 years ago).** The river lords, the hill lords and the port towns swore to Ebba Ardane at the island Chapterhouse of Stillwater, and the Grey Chapter anointed her from the Book of Crowns. Since then no ruler has been lawful without the Chapter's anointing. The Chapter has refused it twice, and both times there was war.
- **The Hill Rising (31 years ago).** House Calder rose against Aldwin's father over taxes and grazing rights, and was broken at Ashby Ford. As the price of peace, Princess Hesper Ardane, Aldwin's sister, was married to the Calder heir. Her son Edric is therefore the dead king's nephew.
- **Aldwin's reign (22 years).** Aldwin married Aveline Marrow, niece of Lady Maud Marrow, binding the river country to the crown. He was careful, cold and good with money, and he trusted Osric Dunmore more than his lords. The realm was peaceful and resentful.
- **The ward (12 years ago).** When House Ostry was slow to send soldiers to the southern coast, Aldwin took a child of the Warden of the Marches to court as a ward: a polite hostage. That child grew up in the Ashen Hall.
- **The lodge fire (5 years ago).** Prince Edmer, then fourteen, went hunting in the northern marches. The lodge near the Last Watchtower burned in the night. A body too burned to know was brought south and buried as the prince. Queen Aveline has worn grey ever since.
- **The dry years (last 3 years).** Little rain has fallen in the north. The great northern fen, called the Drownings, has shrunk for the first time in living memory, and old stonework has begun to rise out of the mud.
- **The king's death (19 days before the story).** Aldwin sickened for a month, grew thin and confused, and died. The royal physician, Master Lucan Fell, disappeared the same night.

## 3. Geography and travel
Wyckholm sits where the high road meets the river Sallow. From the capital:
- **North along the high road:** Greywater Waystation (half a day), then Vane Holdfast (two days from the capital). The Chapterhouse of Stillwater stands on an island in the grey lake beside the waystation; a ferry crosses at dawn and dusk.
- **Further north:** the Last Watchtower (two days past Vane), then Coldharrow, seat of House Ostry (two more days), then the Drownings and the Risen Town (one day each). Snow still lies in the north when the story begins.
- **Down the Sallow by barge:** Sallowford, seat of House Marrow (two days), then Saltreach, the great port of House Lisle (three more days). The Gilded House, the Lisle palace, stands on the headland above the harbour.
- **West along the old road:** Lord Edric Calder's army is camped at Ashby Ford, one day's march from the Lantern Gate. Highcrag, the Calder seat, lies three days further into the hills.
- Inside Wyckholm, every district is a short walk from Tallow Market.

When the player travels between regions, advance time to match these distances. A rider pushing hard can cut a journey by a third, at a cost to horse and rider. Roads are watched: the Regency tolls the high road, Calder outriders patrol the west, and river barges are searched at Sallowford.

## 4. Wyckholm, the capital
A crowded, smoky city of forty thousand, famous for its lamps. By old law every street must be lit from dusk until the night bell, and tallow-rendering is the city's great trade, so the air smells of fat and smoke.
- **The Lantern Gate:** the great north gate, hung with iron lanterns. Everyone who enters gives their name to the Lantern Guard.
- **Tallow Market:** the heart of the city. Chandlers, moneylenders, ballad-singers, pickpockets. The Lisle counting-house, run by Jessa Coyle, stands on its east side.
- **The Ashen Hall:** the royal palace, rebuilt in black stone after a fire a century ago; the soot was never scrubbed from the great hall's beams. The royal archive is in its north tower.
- **The Council Chamber:** a round room under a glass dome inside the Ashen Hall, where the Council of Claims will sit.
- **The Undercroft:** the old cellars and dungeons beneath the Ashen Hall. A forgotten passage comes up in a tallow-renderer's cellar in the market.
- **Sallow Quay:** the river docks. Barges, eel-sellers, customs men, smugglers.
- **The Wicks:** the poor quarter behind the docks, a warren of lodging houses and taverns (the Drowned Lamb is the best known). Sellswords, and people who want to vanish, go there.
- **The Grey Chapel:** the Chapter's house in the city, a plain grey building where petitions are received and the poor are fed.

## 5. The powers
Each power wants something, fears something and has something to trade. None is simply good or evil.

**The Regency** (sigil: an open ledger beneath a lamp). Osric Dunmore controls the treasury, the Lantern Guard and the city. He wants the Council of Claims to choose a weak king who needs him, or, failing that, to delay forever. He fears the great houses uniting against him, and any proof of what really happened to the king. He trades offices, pardons, forgiven debts and silence.

**House Marrow** (sigil: a white heron on green). River-country lords, rich from grain, eels and the tolls of the Sallow crossing. Lady Maud Marrow has ruled the house for thirty years. Publicly she supports the Regency and her niece, the dowager queen. She wants Marrow to stay indispensable to whoever wins, and fears being connected to the king's death. She trades grain, barges and old favours, and never forgets a debt in either direction.

**House Calder** (sigil: a black ram on red). Proud, poor, martial hill lords. Lord Edric Calder has the strongest blood claim and four thousand soldiers camped at Ashby Ford. His mother, Lady Hesper, is the sharpest political mind in the realm. Calder wants the crown, and respect after thirty years as the defeated house. They fear being beaten at the council by coin and law instead of steel. They trade soldiers, protection and future offices.

**House Vane** (sigil: a silver weathervane on blue). A small, old house whose holdfast guards the high road where it narrows between two hills. Whoever holds Vane Holdfast controls the grain carts and couriers between the capital and the north. Vane has two hundred soldiers, little money and a good name. Lord Harlan Vane served in the Lantern Guard in his youth and was once close to the king.

**House Lisle** (sigil: a gold ship on sea-blue). Merchant-princes of Saltreach whose ships trade with lands across the sea. They hold the Regency's debts, and half the lords' debts too. Lord Orlo Lisle wants a king who will honour those debts and grant Lisle the royal salt monopoly. Lisle fears a war that closes the river. They trade loans, ships, foreign goods, and information bought in every port.

**House Ostry** (sigil: three black pines on white). Wardens of the northern marches, trusted by no one in the south. They guard the realm against fen raiders and endure long winters. Warden Hakon Ostry wants the north left alone and his child, held at court as a royal ward, sent home. Ostry fears the southern houses deciding the north's fate without it. They trade furs, hardy soldiers and, lately, strange relics from the Drownings, sold quietly through Lisle.

**The Grey Chapter** (sign: an unmarked grey book). The realm's faith and the keeper of its law. Its members wear undyed grey, take vows of poverty, and are addressed as Brother, Sister or Mother. They teach that the Unwritten (their name for the divine) judges rulers by whether they keep their oaths. They keep the Book of Crowns, which records every lawful ruler's anointing, and only a ruler anointed from it is lawful. Mother Idony leads the Chapter from Stillwater. The Chapter wants the realm to follow its law rather than the strongest army, and fears becoming a prize to be captured. It trades legitimacy, the loyalty of common people, and sanctuary: any Chapter house shelters anyone who asks, and violating sanctuary is a grave crime.

## 6. Everyday life and customs
- **Money:** gold marks, with silver and copper for small dealings. A labourer earns about 1 mark a week, a sellsword 3. A night's lodging costs a few coppers, a good horse 40 marks, a bribe to a gate guard 1–3 marks; buying a minor lord's support costs 100 or more. The player's gold is counted in marks.
- **Titles:** knights are "Sir", lords "my lord", ladies "my lady". The regent is "Lord Regent", the dowager queen still "Your Grace", the head of House Ostry "Warden". Chapter members are Brother, Sister or Mother.
- **Oaths:** sworn on bare steel or before the Chapter. Breaking a Chapter-witnessed oath is a crime as well as a sin, and ruins a reputation for good.
- **The proving:** any noble may demand a proving, a duel before witnesses, to settle a matter of honour. It is legal but frowned upon, and the Lantern Guard breaks up provings inside the city walls.
- **Law:** in the capital the Regency's magistrates judge; elsewhere each lord judges their own lands. Theft and murder are hanged; traitors are beheaded in Tallow Market.
- **Food and drink:** barley bread, eel pie, mutton, river fish, small beer, and the red wine Lisle imports from across the sea. In the north, smoked fish and a bitter pine spirit.
- **Messages:** riders and river barges carry letters; nothing moves faster than a horse. Sealed letters are often opened and resealed by the Regency's clerks.
- **Sayings:** people swear "by the Unwritten" and say "the book is open" to mean the future is undecided. Lamps are left burning for the dead for nine nights.

## 7. The north and the Drownings
The northern marches are pine forest, black rock and cold fen. The fen-folk live in stilted villages in the Drownings, pay no taxes, and are called raiders by the south. As the fen shrinks, the ruins of a town older than Hallan have surfaced: the Risen Town, with worn carvings of sleeping figures on every wall.

In its mud, fen-folk and Ostry scavengers have found bone-glass: pale, smooth fragments that are warm to the touch. People who sleep holding bone-glass dream vividly of places they have never been, and some claim those dreams have come true. The fen-folk say the Sleepers are waking. The Chapter calls bone-glass a superstition, and quietly buys every piece it can find.

**Rules for the uncanny:** there are no spells, monsters or visible sorcery in Hallan. The strange things of the north are always deniable: dreams, uncanny luck, lights over the fen, animals behaving oddly. Never confirm or explain them, and never let bone-glass give the player a reliable power.

## 8. Voice and style
- Grounded political dark fantasy. Rooms are cold and roads muddy. Power works through debts, marriages, letters and fear more often than swords; when violence comes it is quick, ugly and has consequences.
- Characters want things and act even when the player is not watching. Rumours travel, and houses react to what the player does within days.
- Minor people should feel specific: a toll-keeper with chilblains, a Chapter novice who stammers. They stay unnamed, called by their trade ("the ferryman").
- Keep the clock in view: the coming council, the camped army, the season turning.

## 9. Secrets (narrator only)
These are true. Reveal them only through play: evidence found, bargains made, confessions forced or given. Never state a secret in narration before the player has earned it, and never let a character reveal something they couldn't know. When the player learns one, record it with set_flag using the flag name given.

1. **The king was poisoned** (`secret_king_poisoned`). Aldwin was killed with widow's lace, a pale herb from across the sea, given in his wine over a month. Its signs are blue-tinged fingernails, confusion and thinning hair. Lady Maud Marrow arranged it through the king's cupbearer, Emmet Crane, who was found drowned in the Sallow three days after the king died. She did it because Aldwin meant to set her niece Aveline aside and marry a Lisle daughter. The herb came in on a Lisle ship, and Jessa Coyle's ledgers record the cargo without knowing what it was.
2. **The Regent knows** (`secret_osric_blackmails_maud`). Osric discovered the poisoning after the death. He is keeping Master Lucan Fell, the physician, alive in the deepest cell of the Undercroft as proof, and uses that proof to hold Maud Marrow's support. Fell will testify for whoever frees him.
3. **The prince is alive** (`secret_prince_alive`). Five years ago, assassins attacked Prince Edmer at the hunting lodge. He survived, badly burned on the left side of his face and hands. King Aldwin, unable to prove who had sent the killers and fearing civil war, had Harlan Vane burn the lodge, bring back a stranger's body, and hide the prince with House Ostry. Not even Queen Aveline was told. Edmer, now nineteen, lives at the edge of the Drownings as a fen-guide called Ash. He is bitter and clever, and not sure he wants the crown. Only Warden Hakon Ostry and Lord Harlan Vane know.
4. **Who sent the assassins** (`secret_hesper_ordered_attack`). Lady Hesper Calder paid for the attack on Edmer to clear the way for her son. Garrow Flint hired the killers. Edric does not know, and would be sickened.
5. **The king's testament** (`secret_testament_exists`). In his last lucid days Aldwin wrote and sealed a testament declaring that Edmer lives and naming him heir. He gave it to Master Quill, who hid it in a false binding in the royal archive and has told no one. Osric's men have searched the archive twice. The Chapter will treat the testament as lawful if its seal is unbroken when it is presented.
6. **Why the Regent summoned House Vane** (`secret_summons_is_a_trap`). Near the end, raving, Aldwin said, "Harlan knows where the boy is." Osric does not believe the prince lives, but he wants whatever Harlan knows, and he wants the Vane heir in the capital as a hostage for the high road.

## 10. Story shape
There is no fixed plot, and the player can pursue any path. But the world moves on its own:
- **Days 1–10:** the houses gather in the capital; Calder's army waits at Ashby Ford; the Regency tightens the gates; rumours about the king's illness spread.
- **Days 11–30:** alliances are bargained and betrayed; someone tries to buy, threaten or kill anyone who knows too much; the Chapter hears petitions; a clash between Calder soldiers and the Lantern Guard grows likely.
- **Days 31–40:** the houses declare their claims, and the Council of Claims meets on day 40. What the player has uncovered, and whom they have bound to them, decides what happens there.
If the player ignores the main conflict, let it unfold around them and let its consequences reach them.

## 11. Origins
- **The Minor Lord (minor-lord):** the player is the elder child and heir of Lord Harlan Vane, and older sibling of Tomas Vane. The story opens at Vane Holdfast with the Regent's summons just arrived. Harlan, who carries secret 3, suspects a trap and will not say why.
- **The Sellsword (sellsword):** the player is a sergeant in the Tallow Company, sixty blades under Captain Dagna Rusk. The Regency hired the company to keep order in the city during the mourning, and has stopped paying. The story opens in Tallow Market with an unpaid contract and too many people willing to hire a sword.
- **The Royal Ward (royal-ward):** the player is a younger child of Warden Hakon Ostry, brought to court at eight and raised in the Ashen Hall, half guest and half prisoner. Queen Aveline was kind to them; Master Quill taught them their letters. With the king dead, nobody is sure whether the ward is still a hostage, or whose. The story opens in the Ashen Hall.
- **The Witness (witness):** the player was apprentice to Master Lucan Fell, the royal physician, and was in the king's bedchamber when he died. They noticed his blue fingernails, and that night their master vanished. Someone has been asking after the apprentice. The story opens in the Wicks, where the player is hiding.
